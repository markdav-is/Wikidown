# Aerun: The Desert Continent

*A comprehensive guide to the central continent of Vermoon, its political structure, the deep desert, and the secret ecology of the Spice.*

## Geography: The Concentric Rings

Aerun is the central equatorial continent of Vermoon, characterized by a concentric geographical structure. The environment grows progressively more hostile the further inland one travels, creating three distinct zones of habitation and culture.

### 1. The Coastal Ring and The City-States

The outer edge of Aerun is a habitable coastal ring dominated by seven great city-states. These cities control the global commerce of Vermoon, serving as the essential shipping hubs between the northern continent (Lafdea), the Scarlands, and the other landmasses.

The cities are connected by the **Ring Road**, a massive literal highway that circles the entire continent, facilitating the movement of bulk goods that cannot be transported via the Aura network's teleportation systems. While Jak (the Administrator) maintains small temples in these cities, the true power lies with the noble houses that control the trade routes. Each city-state is dominated by one of the seven great merchant houses:

| City-State | Ruling House | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Balic** | House Wavir | Merchant-lord democracy; silt harbor fleet; salt and silver trade |
| **Draj** | House Tsalaxa | Theocratic warrior culture; god-king figurehead; hemp and grain exports |
| **Gulg** | House Inika | Forest city; sorcerer-queen styles herself a goddess; druidic templars |
| **Nibenay** | House Shom | Shadow King rules in mystery; half-giant legions; rare wood exports |
| **Raam** | House M'ke | Teetering on civil war after sorcerer-queen's death; armed factions vie for control |
| **Tyr** | House Vordon | The Free City; first to abolish slavery; council-governed; iron mines |
| **Urik** | House Stel | Militarized warrior-state; sorcerer-king still in power; sealed borders |

Each city is a distinct culture shaped by centuries under its ruler's influence. Despite the differences in law, architecture, and custom, all seven share the same brutal social hierarchy and the same dependency on Spice and water — the two commodities that make life on Aerun possible.

### 2. The Rim Wall West

Separating the coastal ring from the interior is a massive geological barrier known as the Rim Wall. It is the final boundary of civilization. Beyond the Rim Wall, the Aura network fails completely, rendering Jak blind to everything that happens in the interior. The coastal citizens consider everything beyond the wall to be uninhabitable wasteland.

### 3. The Deep Desert and the Tablelands

The center of Aerun is the Deep Desert — a realm of capricious, violent sandstorms and absolute water scarcity. It is entirely off the grid. To the coastal cities, it is a dead zone. To the deep desert tribes (the Fremen-analogues like Kaelen al-Hajra), it is home, a place of harsh religious discipline and survival.

Between the Rim Wall and the true Deep Desert lie the **Tablelands**, a transitional zone of varied and hostile terrain that serves as the hunting ground for raiders, nomads, and desperate travelers:

- **Stony Barrens:** The most common terrain type — vast stretches of exposed bedrock and loose stones, scoured clean by relentless wind. Almost nothing grows here.
- **Sandy Wastes:** Rolling dunes of yellow and orange sand that shift constantly, burying ancient ruins and swallowing the unwary.
- **Rocky Badlands:** Mazes of eroded stone pillars and deep canyons. Raiders use these as lairs, striking at caravans from hidden vantage points.
- **Scrub Plains:** Rare areas that support tough, thorny vegetation. Often fiercely protected by desert druids who will use deadly force to prevent defilers from stripping the land.
- **The Silt Sea:** In certain deep basins, the sand gives way to a sea of fine, powdery dust that flows like water. It is unnavigable by normal means; specialized silt skimmers using psionic levitation are required to cross it. Horrific creatures — silt horrors, mudfiends, and giants — lurk beneath the dust, waiting to drag travelers under.

## The Secret Ecology: Spores, Worms, and Spice

The defining truth of Aerun — known only to the deep desert tribes and hidden from both the coastal nobles and Jak himself — is that the Deep Desert is not a natural phenomenon. It is a **quarantine zone**.

### The First Source

Buried somewhere in the Deep Desert is the "First Source" — an ancient, catastrophic origin point for a fungal/spore infection identical to the one currently emerging from the crashed ship in the Barrier Peaks. The desertification of Aerun was the mechanism of containment. The desert did not happen to Aerun; it was *made*, at terrible cost, to keep something buried.

### The Lifecycle

The ecology of the Deep Desert is a closed loop that keeps the First Source contained:

1. **Spawn:** Spores spawn from the First Source.
2. **Mass:** Without anything organic to attach to in the barren desert, the spores gather into dense masses.
3. **Bloom:** The mass eventually explodes into a violent fungal bloom.
4. **Consumption:** The dead spores and fungal matter are consumed by the apex predators of the deep desert: the great worms.

### The Worms

The coastal cities are aware of smaller, juvenile worms that occasionally breach the Rim Wall, viewing them as dangerous but manageable desert fauna. The massive, ancient worms of the Deep Desert — the true consumers of the spore blooms — are considered myth by the city-dwellers. To the deep desert tribes, they are revered forces of nature that maintain the balance of the world. Without the worms, the cycle breaks and the desert itself would eventually be consumed.

### The Spice and The Way

The byproduct of the worms consuming the dead spores is the **Spice**.

- **The Economy:** Spice is the most valuable resource on Vermoon, generating immense wealth for the coastal noble houses who export it globally.
- **The Effect:** Consumption of the Spice enables and amplifies psionic abilities — known as "the Will and the Way." While almost all beings on Aerun possess minor psionic wild talents due to ambient Spice exposure, concentrated consumption allows users to become true masters of the Way.
- **The Mark:** Heavy, sustained use of the Spice stains the sclera and iris of the user's eyes a deep, uniform blue — an unmistakable sign of a serious practitioner.

The coastal nobles and global consumers know that Spice comes from Aerun, but they believe it is a naturally occurring mineral or botanical resource. The fact that it is the processed byproduct of an apocalyptic fungal infection and giant desert worms is the most closely guarded secret of the deep desert tribes.

## Magic: Preservers and Defilers

On Aerun, arcane magic is intrinsically tied to the life force of the planet. Because of the desertification and the ancient quarantine, plant life is precious beyond measure. Wizards are divided into two distinct camps based on how they draw their power:

**Preservers** have a deep respect for life. They carefully draw only enough energy from surrounding plants to cast their spells, ensuring the flora survives and recovers. Because all magic is viewed with suspicion by the populace, Preservers often operate in secret cells known as the **Veiled Alliance** — a network of underground agents who protect one another and work quietly toward restoring Aerun's lost greenery.

**Defilers** care only for power. When they cast spells, they rip the life force from the surrounding area, turning fertile soil to sterile ash and instantly killing plant life in a visible radius. The sorcerer-kings of the city-states are defilers of the highest order, combining their destructive magic with powerful psionics to maintain their god-like status. Their centuries of defiling are one of the primary reasons the Tablelands are as barren as they are.

Because of the destruction caused by defilers, arcane magic is widely feared and often illegal in the city-states, punishable by death or enslavement.

## Society and Factions

The societies of the coastal ring are rigidly stratified and brutal, reflecting the harshness of the continent itself.

### The Social Hierarchy

The basic structure of city-state society, from top to bottom:

| Tier | Group | Role |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sorcerer-Kings** | God-like immortal rulers; masters of defiling magic and psionics |
| 2 | **Templars** | Bureaucratic enforcers; granted minor magic by their king; manage water distribution |
| 3 | **Nobility / Merchant Houses** | Control farms, water supplies, Spice, and the Ring Road |
| 4 | **Free Citizens** | Artisans, crafters, minor merchants; freedom is tenuous |
| 5 | **Slaves** | The economic foundation; field workers, miners, gladiators |

In cities where the sorcerer-king has fallen — such as Tyr and Raam — this hierarchy is in violent flux. The power vacuum left behind has emboldened noble houses, freed slaves, and ambitious warlords alike.

### Gladiators and The Arenas

In nearly every city-state, massive arenas serve as the primary source of entertainment and social control. Gladiators are highly prized slaves, trained from birth or captured in battle. They fight vicious desert beasts and each other in bloody spectacles. A successful gladiator can earn wealth, fame, and occasionally their freedom — making the arena one of the only paths of social mobility available to the enslaved.

### The Races of Aerun

The harsh environment has shaped the peoples of Aerun into forms distinct from those found elsewhere on Vermoon:

- **Humans:** The dominant race in the city-states, highly adaptable and ruthlessly ambitious.
- **Elves:** Nomadic desert runners and herders who live and die by their speed. Often viewed with suspicion in the cities, known as untrustworthy merchants and thieves — a reputation they have largely earned.
- **Dwarves:** Hairless and intensely focused on a single life-goal or "focus." They are builders and laborers of unmatched dedication, and will pursue their focus to the death.
- **Muls:** A sterile crossbreed between humans and dwarves, bred specifically for the gladiatorial arenas and heavy labor. They possess the size and ambition of humans combined with the incredible stamina of dwarves.
- **Thri-Kreen:** Giant, mantis-like insectoids who roam the deep wastes in hunting packs. They view all other races as potential prey and are perfectly adapted to the desert environment. Rarely seen in the cities; when they are, they are either slaves or attached to an adventuring group they have adopted as their pack.
- **Half-Giants:** Massive, incredibly strong beings often employed as elite guards or shock troops by the sorcerer-kings. Emotionally volatile and easily influenced, they tend to adopt the moral outlook of whoever commands them.

## Political Dynamics: The Balance of Power

The political landscape of Vermoon is defined by the tension between two absolute monopolies.

### Jak and the Aura Network

Jak, ruling from the black stone throne in Greenfjord (in the Scarlands), controls the **Aura Network**. This gives him a monopoly on rapid travel (teleportation) and instant global communication. He is the Emperor figure, seeing and hearing everything that happens on the grid. Beyond the Rim Wall, however, the Aura network fails completely — and Jak is blind.

### The Noble Alliance vs. The Sorcerer-Kings

While the sorcerer-kings rule their individual cities with iron fists, the Noble Houses control the connective tissue between them — the Ring Road, the Spice trade, and the bulk shipping that keeps every city alive. They tolerate Jak's small temples in their cities, but use their monopoly on Spice and bulk shipping as leverage against his monopoly on teleportation and communication.

The recent fall of several sorcerer-kings — in Tyr, Raam, Balic, and Draj — has dramatically shifted this balance. The surviving sorcerer-kings of Urik, Gulg, and Nibenay are consolidating power, while the leaderless cities have become arenas of factional conflict. The Noble Houses are quietly forming a Landsraad-style alliance, recognizing that a united front is the only thing that can check both the remaining sorcerer-kings and Jak's growing ambitions simultaneously.

## The Barrier Peaks Connection

The events in the Barrier Peaks (the crashed ship in the Scarlands) represent a catastrophic threat to this entire global balance.

The ship is a **second spore source**. However, because it crashed in the cold, wet, organic-rich environment of the Scarlands mountains:

1. The spores have abundant life to attach to — flora, fauna, humans — meaning they do not need to gather into masses before blooming.
2. There are no desert worms in the Scarlands to consume the blooms.

Without the worms to close the lifecycle, the fungal infection in the Barrier Peaks will spread unchecked, eventually consuming the Scarlands and potentially the world. Kaelen al-Hajra, hailing from the Deep Desert of Aerun, recognizes the infection immediately. She knows that the only thing keeping her own continent from being consumed is the brutal ecology of the worms and the desert — an ecology the Scarlands does not have. The party's actions in the Barrier Peaks are not merely a local adventure. They are, unknowingly, fighting the same war Aerun has been fighting for millennia.
